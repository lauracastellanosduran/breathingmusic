# Breathing Room TV — ambient long-form master rules

Version 1.0. Active and authoritative for this production cycle.

## Purpose

Create premium long-form ambient videos for the Breathing Room TV YouTube channel.

These videos are designed to accompany a real activity:

- focused work
- studying
- reading
- relaxing
- decompressing after work
- meditation
- sleep / winding down

The content must feel intentional, calm, premium and useful.

Do not create generic "relaxing music" videos simply to fill a publishing schedule.

Every video must answer: what is this video helping the viewer do?

## 1. Content role

Breathing Room has three content systems:

- Shorts: immediate micro-interventions
- Guided long form: guided breathing / meditation experiences
- Ambient long form: music and atmosphere accompanying an activity

Ambient videos must not contain spoken introductions, guided meditation voice-over, breathing instructions, motivational speeches, affirmations, subscribe interruptions, mid-video calls to action, or unnecessary text overlays.

The experience begins immediately.

## 2. Core ambient content pillars

Every video must belong to one primary purpose.

### Focus

Viewer use cases: working, studying, reading, writing, concentrating, deep work.

Music: stable, unobtrusive, low-to-medium energy, minimal melodic distraction, no vocals, restrained rhythm, slow musical development, consistent volume.

Possible visual worlds: rain on window, quiet workspace, slow clouds, forest, soft daylight, evening city window, gentle water, subtle abstract motion.

### Relax

Viewer use cases: after work, mental reset, quiet evening, taking a break, decompressing.

Music: warm, spacious, slow, gentle, low energy, minimal percussion, soft harmonic movement.

Possible visual worlds: forest stream, sunset, gentle ocean, rain, warm interior, trees moving slowly, reflections, desert horizon.

### Meditate

Viewer use cases: meditation, mindful sitting, breathing practice, quiet reflection.

Music: extremely spacious, minimal, slow evolving, low energy, no strong beat, no catchy melody, no dramatic progression, no vocals.

Possible visual worlds: still water, mist, forest light, slow clouds, subtle particles, horizon, very slow abstract movement.

### Sleep / wind down

Viewer use cases: preparing for sleep, nighttime relaxation, quieting the environment, winding down.

Music: very low energy, dark/warm tone, extremely gradual development, no percussion, no sudden musical events, no bright high-frequency events, no climax, no vocals.

Possible visual worlds: rain at night, moonlight, dark ocean, nighttime clouds, dim window, low-light forest, subtle dark abstract movement.

## 3. One video = one purpose

Never combine multiple conflicting purposes.

Good titles name one use:

- 30 Minutes of Calm Focus Music for Work
- Quiet Evening Music to Unwind After Work
- 30 Minutes of Ambient Music for Meditation
- Peaceful Background Music for Reading

The viewer should immediately understand when and why they would use the video.

## 4. Topic selection

Do not randomly select topics. Generate candidate concepts before production.

For every production cycle:

1. Generate 10 candidate video concepts.
2. Assign each a purpose, viewer situation, search intent, music direction, and visual world.
3. Score each candidate.

| Criterion | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| Search / viewer demand | 25 |
| Channel relevance | 25 |
| Specificity | 15 |
| Repeat-use potential | 15 |
| Visual / audio distinctiveness | 10 |
| Library gap | 10 |
| Total | 100 |

Only produce concepts scoring 75 or above. Do not force production if no concept reaches the threshold.

## 5. Title concept before production

Define the viewer promise before generating music. The audio and visual must then be generated for this concept. Do not generate random music first and invent a title afterward.

## 6. Default video duration

Initial testing duration: 30 minutes. Target final duration: 29:30–31:00.

Do not extend a weak composition simply to reach 30 minutes. Do not create an obvious short loop repeated many times.

## 7. ElevenLabs music generation

Use ElevenLabs as the music-generation provider. Set `force_instrumental` true. No vocals. Generate music specifically for the selected purpose.

The prompt must define viewer activity, emotional tone, energy, instrumentation, melodic density, rhythmic density, evolution, and forbidden characteristics.

## 8. 30-minute music construction

Do not repeat one short audio file six times. Build a continuous 30-minute experience.

Preferred architecture: section A, section B, and section C, each about 8–10 minutes. All sections must belong to the same sonic world. They should feel like developments of one experience, not three unrelated songs.

Use long smooth crossfades between sections. Default crossfade: 10–20 seconds. No audible hard cuts.

## 9. Musical energy curve

Ambient content should not follow intro, build, climax, end.

- Focus: stable, stable, slightly calmer
- Relax: calm, calmer, calmest
- Meditation: spacious, deeper, spacious
- Sleep: calm, lower energy, very low energy

Never increase intensity substantially near the end.

## 10. Audio continuity

Before accepting the 30-minute master, verify that sections sound like the same musical world and that there are no obvious restart points, abrupt key or tempo changes, sudden instrumentation changes, volume jumps, unexpected vocals, audio glitches, harsh frequencies, accidental silence, or a sudden ending.

## 11. Ending audio

Do not end abruptly. The final 30–60 seconds should naturally become slightly simpler or remain stable. Apply a gentle final fade. Avoid a dramatic musical resolution.

## 12. Visual philosophy

The visual supports the experience. The music is the primary product. Visuals should create a place the viewer is comfortable leaving on screen for 30 minutes.

Preferred categories: nature, atmospheric environments, premium abstract motion, and occasional cosmic or space. Nature should be the primary visual language. Cosmic visuals must remain occasional.

## 13. Visual movement

Prefer slow natural movement: rain, moving clouds, leaves, water reflections, gentle ocean, stream, fog, sunlight, subtle shadows, slow particles.

Avoid rapid drone footage, crashing waves, waterfalls with aggressive motion, timelapses, fast camera movement, dramatic zooms, rapid cuts, busy crowds, distracting people, high-energy city footage, flashing light, and obvious short stock loops.

## 14. Visual continuity

Do not edit long-form ambient content like a music video. Do not change footage every few seconds. Prefer one visual world for the entire video. Multiple shots are acceptable only from the same environment, transitioning very slowly.

## 15. Visual looping

If the source visual is shorter than the final video, create a seamless extension with long crossfades, an alternate crop, subtle speed variation, reversed sections only when physically believable, or slow digital movement when appropriate. The loop must not call attention to itself.

## 16. Visual energy curve

Visual energy must remain stable or decrease. Default: calm, calm, calmer. Never introduce a visually dramatic sequence near the end. The final 20% should have equal or lower visual energy than the preceding section.

## 17. Text on screen

Do not leave titles or instructions permanently on screen. Optional opening identification may last about 3–5 seconds, then no text. Do not display motivational quotes, breathing instructions, lyrics, repeated branding, subscribe prompts, or timers by default.

## 18. Branding

Breathing Room branding should be subtle. An optional small logo may appear during the opening or closing. Do not place a large permanent watermark in the center.

## 19. Thumbnail

The thumbnail must communicate the use case quickly. Use one strong visual. Keep text minimal, about 2–5 words. Duration may be communicated through the title rather than the thumbnail.

## 20. Titles

Titles should prioritize use case, context, and duration when useful. Avoid clickbait, all-caps titles, excessive emojis, and unsupported medical claims.

## 21. Description

Keep the description natural and useful:

```
A quiet 30-minute ambient session for [USE CASE].

Designed for [CONTEXT / ACTIVITY], with [SHORT DESCRIPTION OF MUSIC AND ATMOSPHERE].

No talking. No interruptions.

Breathing Room — a little space for whatever you're going through.
```

Then add appropriate channel links or playlist information if available.

## 22. Playlists

Categorize videos into Breathing Room — Focus, Relax, Meditate, and Sleep. Do not create unnecessary playlists until enough videos exist.

## 23. Audio QA

Before rendering video, inspect the final audio for duration, clipping, unexpected silence, loudness jumps, abrupt transitions, vocals, corrupted audio, and a smooth ending.

Create QA samples at 00:00, 05:00, 10:00, 15:00, 20:00, 25:00, and 29:30. Human review should listen to samples before publication.

## 24. Video QA

Before publication verify duration, 16:9, audio, picture throughout, no black frames, no broken loops, no visible stock watermark, no abrupt visual cuts, no accidental text, opening, ending, thumbnail, title, and description.

Generate preview frames at 00:05, 05:00, 10:00, 15:00, 20:00, 25:00, and 29:30.

## 25. Initial publishing mode

Do not automatically publish publicly. Render locally first. Produce `FINAL_VIDEO.mp4`, `THUMBNAIL.jpg`, `metadata.json`, `audio_master.mp3`, and `qa_report.json`. Set upload status to private until the workflow has repeatedly passed human QA.

## 26. Performance metrics

Do not evaluate these videos using Shorts metrics. Primary metrics are watch hours, average view duration, average percentage viewed, returning viewers, impressions, click-through rate, traffic source, YouTube search terms, and subscribers gained. Absolute views alone are not enough.

## 27. Learning system

For every published video store the video id, date, purpose, viewer situation, title, duration, music profile, visual world, thumbnail style, and the performance metrics above. Do not change strategy based on one video's performance.

## 28. Replication rule

When a concept performs strongly, do not simply duplicate it. Identify why it may have worked, preserve the viewer intent, and introduce a meaningful variation.

## 29. Non-repetition

Track previous titles, topics, music prompts, visuals, and thumbnail concepts. Avoid publishing near-identical videos consecutively. Variation must come from viewer situation, sonic palette, visual world, time of day, environment, or specific activity.

## 30. Final decision

Before producing any ambient video, answer:

1. What exactly is the viewer doing while this plays?
2. Why would they choose this video rather than generic ambient music?
3. Does the music support that activity?
4. Does the visual support the same emotional environment?
5. Can the viewer comfortably leave this running for 30 minutes?
6. Is there any moment likely to interrupt concentration, relaxation, or meditation?

If the answer to the first question is unclear, do not produce. If the answer to the sixth is yes, fix that before publishing.

## Golden rule

Breathing Room ambient content is not AI music plus random stock footage plus 30 minutes.

It is a specific purpose, a coherent sound world, a coherent visual world, and an uninterrupted experience.

The viewer should forget the video is there, while still wanting to keep it playing.
